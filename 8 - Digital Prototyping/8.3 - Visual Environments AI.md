### 8.3 Visual Environments

In the prototype, the physical precinct is not a decorative backdrop; it is part of the epistemic system. Space gates access to files, distributes clues across institutional stations, stages shifting social encounters, and imposes temporal friction on every movement between leads. Answering the open threads from Chapter 7 (§7.1, §7.2.3, and §7.4.1), this section details how the tabletop floor plan was translated into a navigable 3D precinct (`GeometryAttempt`), how doors and room-level visibility make game state legible in space, and how camera framing, lighting, and auxiliary scenes (`Dream_Sequence`, `Ending_Sequence`, and `MainMenu`) shape play.

#### 8.3.1 From Four Tabletop Rooms to a Navigable Precinct

The Chapter 6 tabletop workshops relied on four abstract spaces drawn on paper: the Evidence Room, Corridor, Observation Room, and Interrogation Room (Chapter 6, Figure 38). During those rehearsals, participants repeatedly tested doors and asked to inspect adjacent areas that were not yet modelled (§7.1). To satisfy that spatial curiosity while keeping solo-project scope bounded, the digital prototype preserves the four core rooms and expands the precinct floor (`GeometryAttempt`) to **six rooms** plus the Jail Room from the `Ending_Sequence` Scene:

1. **Evidence Room (`RoomGroup_ER`)**
2. **Corridor (`RoomGroup_CR`)**
3. **Observation Room (`RoomGroup_OR`)**
4. **Interrogation Room (`RoomGroup_IR`)**
5. **Bathroom (`RoomGroup_BR`)** — added to give the player an isolated, non-public space for administering Somnoryl-μ.
6. **Locked Room (`RoomGroup_LR`)** — added as Detective Thorne’s sealed case archive, housing the deeper paper trail and the physical collapse sequence.

Following the scoping principle set out in §7.1, every location exists to serve a specific narrative and epistemic function rather than to pad floor space. During pre-production, a purely 2D visual-novel presentation was rejected for three reasons: it could not easily convey the spatial scale of a precinct, it demanded unsustainable 2D character and background illustration variants as suspects and room states changed, and it removed self-directed spatial exploration. Adopting a real-time 3D **isometric-style** perspective—an elevated three-quarter view rather than a rigid, fixed classic orthographic projection—offered an economical alternative: a single modular environment could support free movement, physical sightlines, diegetic clue placement, and reusable character animations within a realistic solo development budget.

#### 8.3.2 What Each Room/Scene Does

##### `MainMenu`

**`MainMenu`:** Presents the title interface over a full-screen `VideoPlayer` cutscene pipeline (`MainMenuController`), with `MainMenuTheme` and `RainLoop` objects present (inactive in the base scene hierarchy).

##### `GeometryAttempt`

**Evidence Room (`RoomGroup_ER`):** The player begins the investigation here, framed as Detective Julian Stoker’s workspace. It exposes the primary investigative tools: two computer terminal interactables (`slavictv/Slavic_Tv_Mesh` and `Desk_Computer_ER`) running `ComputerTerminalUI`, a tape container holding the four institutional access tapes (`FL`, `MU`, `EC`, `CSU`), a physical evidence box (`storage_box_TOP`) containing six foundational pickups (`E1`, `E2`, `E3`, `E15`, `E13`, `E17`), a resting chair (`chair_ER_00`) wired to `TimeAdvanceTrigger`, a quick-access reading folder (`THIS`), and a window casting real-time blue exterior illumination paired with 3D spatialized rain audio (`Sounds_ER`) whose volume rises as Julian approaches the glass and fades as he moves away. (A starter `paperstack_ER` pickup is disabled in the build, as starter files are granted automatically after Miller’s opening briefing.) While the Evidence Room opens freely onto the Corridor, it withholds the Locked Room behind `Door_LR` until the player acquires the keys (`KEYS`).

**Corridor (`RoomGroup_CR`):** The Corridor functions as the spatial hub connecting all five interior rooms and terminating at `Door_STAIRS` (`WorldObject_Stairs_Door`). It exposes no standalone forensic pickups; instead, it mediates movement and houses the precinct’s exit gate. Attempting `Door_STAIRS` before `FLAG_ENDING_SEQUENCE_STARTED` is set triggers a `StairsDoorHandler` fallback interaction telling the player the case is not yet ready, withholding the transition to `Ending_Sequence` until the investigation reaches closure.

**Observation Room (`RoomGroup_OR`):** Positioned beside the Interrogation Room, the Observation Room represents the station’s institutional channel. It stages Captain Miller’s presence (`MillerRoot`) and Officer Buckley (`BuckleyRoot`, offering guidance actions via `WorldObject_Buckley`), alongside a second `TimeAdvanceTrigger` (`printer_OR`) and a quick-access documents folder (`documentsfolder_OR (1)`). Visually overlooking the interrogation space, it reinforces the procedural hierarchy watching over Stoker’s work.

**Interrogation Room (`RoomGroup_IR`):** Rather than remaining static, the Interrogation Room’s contents change dynamically across play. Its door (`DoorHinge_IR`) is controlled by a `FlagDrivenDoor` bound to `FLAG_SUSPECT_IN_IR`, withholding physical entry until a suspect is brought in. When Elara (`FLAG_ELARA_IN_IR`) or Kae (`FLAG_KAE_IN_IR`) is summoned, `IRSeatManager` and `CharacterRoomPresence` coordinate their walk-in, seated idle (`NPCSeatedBehaviour`), and eviction animations when suspects are swapped. Alongside the active suspect, the room exposes a quick-access document folder (`documentsfolder_IR (1)`), table, chairs, and cassette recorder.

**Bathroom (`RoomGroup_BR`):** Sealed behind `DoorHinge_BR` (`FLAG_BR_DOOR_OPEN`), the Bathroom can be opened either legitimately with `KEYS` (`Action_Bathrooom_KEYS`) or forced open by combining Somnoryl-μ (`E15`) and the Auto Injector (`AI`) (`Action_Bathrooom_Dream`). Inside, it offers only a flushable toilet (`WorldObject_Toilet`) and privacy. Epistemically, however, its isolation is critical: `SceneReturnHandler` checks the player’s current room upon waking from `Dream_Sequence`. Triggering the dream inside `BathroomRoom` sets `BATHROOM_DREAM_EXPERIENCED` and safely returns Julian to the investigation; triggering it in any public room sets `FLAG_MILLER_ANGRY_SEQUENCE`, forcing an immediate confrontation.

**Locked Room (`RoomGroup_LR`):** Behind `DoorHinge_LR` (`FLAG_LOCKEDDOOR_OPEN`, unlocked with `KEYS` requested from Miller, or offering an optional note-writing action `Action_Locked_Room_LDN`), the Locked Room acts as Thorne’s neglected archive rather than a mechanical puzzle box. It exposes 25 dossier and forensic records (`storage_box_BOT_02`), two prior case folders (`CASE-19` and `CASE-47` in `storage_box_TOP_3` and `documentsfolder_LR`), shelving, filing cabinets, and suspended dust (`AmbientDust`). Crossing its threshold (`Door_Trigger_LR_SEQUENCE`) triggers `LockedRoomEntranceTrigger`, launching Julian’s collapse (`FallSequenceController`) and the 25-second adrenaline injection countdown (`LockedRoomSequenceController`)—turning spatial discovery into embodied narrative jeopardy.

##### Doors as State Made Visible

Doors in the precinct translate the flag architecture of Section 8.1 into immediate spatial feedback. Locked doors do not open by collision; they open only through `WorldObjectInteractor` and `WorldObjectActionData` interactions requiring specific equipped evidence (such as `KEYS` or `E15` + `AI`), which write flags like `FLAG_LOCKEDDOOR_OPEN` or `FLAG_BR_DOOR_OPEN`. Three `FlagDrivenDoor` hinges (`DoorHinge_LR`, `DoorHinge_IR`, and `DoorHinge_BR`) subscribe to `NarrativeStateManager.OnFlagSet` and smoothly rotate open (`-90°` on LR and IR) over `0.5s` when their flag fires; if a flag is already active at scene start—for instance after loading a save—the door initializes directly in its open rotation.

Proximity readability is handled by `InteractableProximityOutline`, which switches an object’s rendering layer to an outline layer when Julian enters its detection radius. Once a `FlagDrivenDoor` opens (or a `WorldObjectInteractor` with `disableWhenExhausted` completes its `disableFlag` condition), its outline component is permanently disabled. In this way, the golden highlight functions as a world-space state indicator: seeing an outline on a doorframe tells the player immediately whether the door remains locked, while its absence confirms that the threshold is already open.

##### Seeing Only Where You Are: Room-Level Visibility

To prevent players from inspecting rooms they have not entered and to keep rendering lightweight, `GameStateManager` activates only one room root at a time (`RoomRootRegistrar`), initializing in `EvidenceRoom`. `RoomTrigger` box colliders embedded in every doorway detect the player entering a threshold and call `GameStateManager.TransitionToRoom(targetRoom)`.

Strict single-room culling, however, would sever visual continuity through open doorways. `RoomTrigger` therefore supports `coVisibleRooms` and `objectsToHide`: stepping into the Interrogation Room keeps `ObservationRoom` visible; stepping into the Bathroom keeps `CorridorRoom` visible; and crossing into the Locked Room keeps `EvidenceRoom` visible. Conversely, unentered rooms are capped by dedicated dark occlusion groups (`FOW`) inside each room hierarchy. Because unentered rooms are deactivated and not rendered, these `FOW` dark caps cleanly cover the outer cutoffs so the player sees only a dark interior void rather than raw geometry seams. Whenever a doorway trigger reveals an adjacent room, it hides the blocking `FOW` object; `HandleRoomChanged` restores it as soon as the player leaves. Upon returning from `Dream_Sequence`, `SceneReturnHandler` restores `DreamSessionData.ReturnRoom` so the player wakes directly inside the room where the injection occurred.

#### Space and Time

Because `InternalClockManager` drains in real time (`Time.deltaTime / 3600` per frame while running), spatial traversal directly consumes the 24-hour *kairos* window introduced in Section 8.1.5. Walking between the Evidence Room, Corridor, and Observation Room costs only seconds of game time, yet repeated backtracking adds up—while the major temporal leaps remain deliberate `TimeAdvanceTrigger` skips at the chair and printer, pending laboratory delays, and the six-hour penalty on returning from the dream. Together, room gating and clock drain turn physical movement into resource management, reinforced visually by the persistent clock HUD discussed in Section 8.2.2.

#### Light, Shadow, and Atmosphere

`GeometryAttempt` contains no active `Directional Light`; the entire precinct is lit by per-room local light groups (`Lights_ER`: 3 lights, `Lights_CR`: 4, `Lights_OR`: 3, `Lights_IR`: 2, `Lights_BR`: 2, `Lights_LR`: 3) paired with per-room `Light Probe Group`s. In the Evidence Room, this interior warmth is contrasted by real-time blue illumination spilling through the exterior window. To support baking under modest hardware constraints, an editor utility (`CreateOmenLightingAsset`) configures a low-spec moody GPU lightmapper profile (`64`/`128`/`64` direct/indirect/environment samples, `1024` maximum lightmap resolution, and OpenImage denoising) *[Author note: confirm whether this generated `LightingSettings` asset is assigned in the final scenes, and insert post-processing / volume configuration details here]*.

Dynamic character grounding relies on `CharacterShadowController` rather than expensive real-time character shadow maps: each character (`Julian`, `Miller`, `Elara`, `Kae`, and `Buckley`) casts a downward ray onto `groundMask` to position, scale, and fade a blob-shadow quad (`BlobShadow_*`) beneath their feet. In the Locked Room, `AmbientDust` (`AtmosphericDustController`, configured at `220.9` normal density and `710.5` disturbed density) reinforces the stagnant, unvisited feel of Thorne’s archive. Both `GeometryAttempt` (`WallsDark`, 9 objects) and `Ending_Sequence` (`DarkenedWalls`, 5 objects) also include darkened wall sets *[Author note: confirm the exact visual/boundary purpose of `WallsDark` and `DarkenedWalls`, e.g., darkened cutoff/perimeter walls framing culled room silhouettes]*.

##### `Ending_Sequence`

- **`Ending_Sequence`:** Reusing `MainArchitecture` from the precinct avoids rebuilding a separate courthouse or prison. Two `JailWall` partitions and cell doors (`Door_Elara`, `Door_Kae`) convert the interrogation wing into holding cells, complemented by `DarkRoof_OR`, five corridor lights (`Light_CR`), `Audio_Source_Ambience`, `Audio_Source_Crying_SFX`, inactive `Elara_Crying` / `Kaelen_Seated` scene props, and `NavySuitChar_Mixamo (1)` for Miller’s closing approach.

##### `Dream_Sequence`

- **`Dream_Sequence`:** Whereas Chapter 5’s concept art (Figure 32) envisioned an expansive surreal void, solo production scope compressed the dream into a tightly bounded chamber (`Floor_02_DS`, four walls `Wall_00_DS`–`Wall_03_DS`, a single `Point Light`, one `Light Probe Group`, and `Audio_Source_Ambience`). Both Julian and the Entity appear as abstract mannequin meshes (`Alpha_Joints`/`Alpha_Surface` and `Beta_Joints`/`Beta_Surface`), stripping away detective clothing and precinct clutter. Walking triggers water footsteps at `0.2` base volume—four times louder than the precinct’s `0.05`—foregrounding the dream as an acoustic and symbolic space rather than a forensic one.

#### 8.3.5 Camera and Framing

`IsometricCamera` tracks Julian from an elevated three-quarter offset of `(-20, 20, 0)` with a slight `lookAtOffset`, rather than a rigid two-axis isometric lock. Following uses a lazy dead zone of `2 × 1.5` units and `0.25s` position smoothing so short adjustments within a workstation feel grounded rather than jerky. Player movement in `PlayerController` is remapped onto the camera’s flattened horizontal axes (`RemapToCameraSpace`), ensuring that pressing *up* always moves Julian away from the camera.

Scroll-wheel zoom operates between `2.5` (`zoomedInSize`) and `5` (`zoomedOutSize`, overridden in `GeometryAttempt` from the script’s fallback default of `9`), and is automatically suppressed whenever `UIPanelTracker.IsAnyUIPanelOpen` or player control is locked. During suspect dialogue and the locked-room fall sequence, `ZoomInForInteraction` shifts the camera `15%` toward the conversational partner (`_interactionFocus`) and eases the orthographic size to `65%` of the default size (approximately `3.25`). The camera explicitly prevents zooming *out* if the player was already zoomed closer (`Mathf.Min(_preInteractionSize, wanted)`), restoring the player’s prior zoom once the interaction ends.
