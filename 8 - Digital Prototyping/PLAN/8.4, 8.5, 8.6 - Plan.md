# 8.5 Sound Design

**Thesis of the section:** sound is a second layer of the epistemic system. The music reacts to flags and the clock, so the player hears the state of the investigation. It is not background decoration. This follows §4.5 and §6.2.1.

### 8.5.1 Role of sound, starting from the workshop

Questions to answer:

- What did the workshop show? Noir-jazz and rain ambience at the start, and a more intense track near session end to signal urgency (§6.2.1).
- What did sound have to do in the digital version that the facilitator did before? The facilitator controlled the music by hand, and the game had to automate that.
- What is the sound rule of the game: no mechanical penalty from the clock (§6.2.5), but an audible one?

What to write:

- A short bridge from the workshop observation to the design requirement, in two or three paragraphs.
- State the three jobs of sound: set the genre, mark time pressure, and mark narrative turning points.

### 8.5.2 Music and tension cues tied to the clock

This is the strongest part of 8.5, because the system is a real design contribution. I resolved the cue → track mapping from the repo. Use these as examples.

Questions to answer:

- How does a cue decide to play? Each cue is a data asset with `requiredFlags`, `blockedByFlags`, a priority, a `forcePlay` option, a `loop` option and a clock gate (`activeWhenHoursRemainingBelow`).
- How are conflicts between cues resolved? A priority queue, force-play for turning points, a "spent" set so a cue is not replayed, and crossfades.
- How is the clock tied in? Example: `CUE_ROUND_MIDNIGHT` (priority 30) plays only when fewer than 15 hours remain, and only while the clock is running. `CUE_TIME_WARNING` (priority 40, force-play) is flag-triggered at the 12h warning.
- What happens on save/load and when the menu opens? `SaveManager` stores the music state and the pause and main-menu controllers pause or reset it.

What to write, as a structure:

- The tier logic, with a figure (a priority ladder works well):
    - Ambient and progression cues: `INTERNAL_CLOCK_STARTS` (5), `N1_ACQUIRED` (6), and a group of dialogue-triggered cues at priority 15–16 (Elara dream, sleepwalking, gun reaction, financial issues, two-shapes theory, Miller angry).
    - Story-beat cues: `LOCKED_ROOM_SEQUENCE_STARTED` (20), `AV_USED` (25), `MOON_USED` (26), `DREAM_EXPERIENCED` (27).
    - Clock cues: `ROUND_MIDNIGHT` (30), `TIME_WARNING` (40).
    - Ending and failure cues: `MILLER_PLAYER_FIRED` and the five `ENDING_*` cues, all at priority 50 with force-play.
- Why the ending cues sit at the top: nothing may interrupt a verdict.
- Examples of track choice: Miles Davis "Something I Dreamed Last Night" on the AV, Hank Mobley "Dam That Dream" for the dream, Herbie Hancock "Maiden Voyage" as the time warning, "Round Midnight" at 15h, Twin Peaks theme for the Kae-murder/Elara-free ending. Note the choices are curated references (noir, jazz, dream-themed titles).
- The `MENUS` cue is priority 40 with no flags, so menus override everything.

Unresolved: three cues point to clips outside the folders I could read (`ELARA_GUN_REACTION`, `KAE_RESPOSIBLE_AUGUST_INCIDENT`, `LOCKED_ROOM_SEQUENCE_STARTED`). Check them in the Inspector before quoting. Also note `CUE_LOCKED_ROOM_SEQUENCE_STARTED` has volume 1 and `MOON_USED` has 0.4, against 0.1 for most. Say whether that is deliberate, because a reader will ask.

### 8.5.4 Mix, settings, and sound as epistemic infrastructure

Questions to answer:

- How does the mix work? A `MasterMixer` with SFX and Music groups, and four player sliders (master, music, SFX, ambience) stored in PlayerPrefs (`vol_master`, `vol_music`, `vol_sfx`, `vol_ambience`). The defaults are 0.8, 0.8, 1.0 and 0.7. Slider values are converted to decibels with 20·log10, and the floor is −80 dB.
- Why does this matter to the argument? The player controls how loud the clock and the story cues are, which means the audible pressure is under the player's control.
- What does sound tell the player without telling them what is true? Cues mark state and mood, not correctness. A cue firing is not a verdict on the player's theory.

What to write:

- A brief technical paragraph on the mixer and settings, and the pause/resume behaviour.
- The epistemic argument: sound as infrastructure (§4.5), with two or three examples from 8.5.2. The key is that the music flags the moment and emotional state, and never confirms the interpretation.
- One honest note: the ambience slider appears in the settings, but I haven't confirmed which mixer group the ambience sources route to. The mixer's exposed parameter names are also unverified. Check both in the AudioMixer before writing this paragraph.
