### 8.2 Interface Design

The user interface is where the prototype’s epistemic principles become directly manipulable. Carrying forward the paper-and-HTML/CSS planning from Chapters 6 and 7, the interface treats evidence as a physical workspace, embeds the two-slot constraint inside conversation rather than behind menus, enforces clear modal focus across overlapping windows, and signals procedural relevance without ever confirming whether the player’s overarching theory is true.

#### 8.2.1 The Interface as the Interrogation Instrument

Rather than hiding evidence selection behind a separate inventory screen, `DialogueUI` (`DialoguePanel.uxml`) places `Slot A` and `Slot B` directly inside the conversation panel (`slots-sidebar`, `160px` wide) alongside the dialogue stream and options list (`main-content`, `726px` wide, anchored bottom-centre). Every time either slot changes, `SlotManager.OnSlotsChanged` triggers `DialogueUI.ReEvaluateQuestions()`, invoking `CombinationEvaluator.Evaluate` and immediately rebuilding the available question buttons. For the player, interrogation becomes an iterative physical test: they arrange one or two clues in the sidebar and immediately observe what lines of questioning the character is willing to entertain.

To keep slot manipulation fast during conversation, the interface supports three ways of equipping evidence without leaving dialogue:
1. **Drag-and-drop:** Cards can be dragged from the panel’s Quick Menu (`quick-menu` / `qm-grid`) onto `slot-drop-A` or `slot-drop-B` via `DragGhostHandler`, which spawns a tilted floating ghost card (`drag-ghost`) and highlights valid drop targets (`.slot-drag-over`), or dragged back out to unequip (`TryUnequipOnDragOut`).
2. **Click-to-equip:** Clicking a card in the Quick Menu when not dragging equips it directly into the target slot.
3. **Quick Menu tab switching:** Clicking an empty slot opens the `quick-menu` drawer beside the dialogue panel, allowing the player to switch between their currently equipped folder (`qm-tab-starter`) and the full inventory (`qm-tab-inventory`), complementing the free-roam Quick Menu (`FreeRoamQuickMenuController`, cycled with `[H]`).

Switching between **Inquiry** and **Confrontation** (`[Q] Switch Mode`) happens inside the same panel rather than opening a separate accusation UI. `DialogueUI.ApplyModeStyle()` toggles the `.mode-confrontation` class on the overlay, re-skinning the mode badge (`#36689b` to `#904c77`), occupied slot borders (`#37699e` to `#904c77`), detective speech accents (`#101820` to `#191020`), and question button hover states from detective slate-blue to confrontation magenta, while character speech remains warm amber (`#af8041`). The player feels the escalation from open questioning to confrontation visually before choosing a line.

Question buttons themselves form a readable visual vocabulary through distinct left-border accents in `DialoguePanel.uss`:
- **Chain and standalone questions** (`.question-btn--chain`, `.question-btn--standalone`) carry the active mode’s blue or magenta accent.
- **Resume options** (`.question-btn--resume`) appear in warm gold (`#d4a94e` border on `#2a2416`), signalling a bookmarked `LogicChainData` thread waiting to be continued.
- **In-chain escape actions** (`.question-btn--action`) use a muted grey border (`#8a8880`) to distinguish UI meta-actions (breaking out of an active chain or switching modes) from in-fiction questions.
- **Locked actions** (`.question-btn--locked`) render at `52%` opacity with a muted red edge (`rgba(255, 80, 80, 0.35)`), signalling world interactions whose required items are not yet equipped.

By default, `main-content` opens collapsed (`353px` high, `conversation-stream` capped at `183px` with no scrollbar), keeping only the latest speech bubble visible so attention stays anchored on the current exchange; clicking the stream expands the panel (`573px`) to reveal prior turns.

#### 8.2.2 One Panel Grammar Across Systems

To ensure the player only needs to learn one interaction grammar, `DialogueUI` unifies four distinct gameplay contexts behind the same bottom-centred panel architecture:

| Panel Mode | Header Badge & Controls | Slots Sidebar | Content Area | Exit / Mode Switch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Dialogue** | `INQUIRY` or `CONFRONTATION` + character ID; `[Q]` toggle visible | Always visible (`Slot A` / `Slot B`) | Speech stream + evaluated chain/standalone/resume buttons | `✕ END CONVERSATION` enabled; `[Q]` enabled |
| **World Object (`INTERACTION`)** | `INTERACTION` badge + object display name; `[Q]` hidden | Shown only if `WorldObjectData.showSlotPanel` is true | Initial `observationText` + `WorldObjectActionData` list | Close button enabled; `[Q]` hidden |
| **Narrative Sequence (`NARRATIVE`)** | `NARRATIVE` badge + page speaker; `[Q]` hidden | Hidden | Linear `NarrativeDialogueData` pages, optional `NarrativeChoice` buttons, `CONTINUE ▼` | Closes on sequence completion |
| **Forced Confrontation** | `CONFRONTATION` + Miller ID; `[Q]` hidden | Visible (`Slot A` / `Slot B`) | Speech stream + Miller confrontation options (`NPCForcedConfrontation`) | Exit button and `[Q]` removed; cannot leave until resolved |

> **Table 8.X — Four Modes of the Unified `DialogueUI` Panel.**

Whether questioning a suspect, inspecting a locked door or Buckley (`WorldObjectInteractor`), reading a triggered briefing (`IntroSequenceController`, `FlagNarrativeManager`), or defending the case during Miller’s forced confrontation (`NPCForcedConfrontation`), spatial layout, button feedback, and slot manipulation remain consistent. The `ComputerTerminalUI` (`ComputerTerminal.uxml`) deliberately extends this same grammar: it reuses `slot-drop-A`, `slot-drop-B`, `DragGhostHandler`, and `EvidenceCardFactory` cards (`BuildCard`, `BuildSlotCard`), adding only a dedicated `ACCESS TAPES` top strip (`tapes-grid` with cyan-bordered `.tape-card` entries) above `INVENTORY` and `EQUIPPED FOLDER` tabs, and ejecting tapes back to the player’s pre-open slot state (`EjectTapesFromSlots`) on close so terminal tapes do not clutter subsequent interrogations.

#### 8.2.3 Evidence as a Workspace for Thinking

Because Chapter 6 showed that players naturally sort, annotate, and cross-check documents between conversations, the interface treats the 61 `EvidenceData` items as an active reasoning workspace rather than a passive inventory list:

- **Inventory and Folders (`InventoryPanelController`):** Opened with `[I]`, the inventory displays all acquired items in a responsive card grid (`evidence-grid`) alongside a folder tab strip managed by `EvidenceFolderManager` (supporting starter and player-created folders, up to six folders, created with `[N]`, renamed with `[T]`, deleted with `[L]` via a confirmation prompt clarifying that evidence remains in the main inventory, and cycled with `[Q]`/`[E]`). Pressing `[W]` toggles the left sidebar between **Slots View** (`variant-slots`) and **Folder Contents View** (`variant-folder`) so players can drag evidence into custom case folders and pin one folder (`EQUIP FOR QM`, marked `★`) to appear in the conversation Quick Menu. Hover-sensitive shortcuts (`[F]` Inspect, `[R]` Read, `[A]`/`[S]` Slot A/B, `[X]` Remove) illuminate when hovering over a card and dim (`.shortcut-item--dimmed`) when idle.
- **Inspect Mode (`InspectModeController`):** Opened with `[F]` or double-click (`EvidenceCardFactory.OnCardDoubleClicked`), Inspect Mode pairs the evidence card and quick-equip buttons (`[W]`, `[A]`, `[S]`, highlighted `.equip-btn--active` when equipped) with a multi-page preview and a personal **Note** box (`note-container` styled with `NoteTexture`). Players can write up to `288` characters per item (`[N]` to edit, `[Enter]` to save); notes persist in `SaveManager` and are never parsed or graded by any game system.
- **Read Mode (`ReadModeController`):** Opened with `[R]` from Inspect Mode, inventory, or quick-access room folders (`ReadModeQuickAccessInteractable`), Read Mode provides a full-screen document reader with `0.5×`–`4×` zoom (`Ctrl+Scroll`), double-click `2×` toggle, drag-panning, `[V]` view reset, page arrows, and edge-hover cycling strips (`read-edge-left`, `read-edge-right`). High-magnification reading ensures dense forensic tables, timestamps, and medical notes remain genuinely legible rather than decorative props.
- **Conversation Archive (`ArchiveUI`):** Opened with `[L]`, the Archive presents a virtualized left `ListView` of past sessions grouped by character (`MILLER`, `ELARA`, `KAE`) and a right conversation transcript stamped with `SLOT A + SLOT B` badges (`slot-badges-bar`) and inline `USED` evidence chips (`entry-evidence-chip`) under detective statements. Mirroring the player’s tabletop notes, it serves as external memory of what was already asked and shown.

Crucially, `GlobalUIInputHandler` binds `EvidenceHoverManager` across panels: pressing `[F]` or `[R]` while hovering over any acquired, non-pending card—whether in the Inventory, Quick Menu, Terminal, or Free-Roam slot strip—opens Inspect or Read Mode directly in that context.

#### 8.2.4 Layering, Focus, and Control

With up to nine UI panels coexisting in `GeometryAttempt`, interface management must guarantee visual order, prevent the detective from walking underneath open windows, and ensure `[Esc]` behaves predictably. Each `UIDocument` sits at an explicit `Sorting Order`:

| Sorting Order | UI Document(s) | Escape Behaviour |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **3** | `FreeRoamUI` (`FreeRoamHUD`) | Non-modal HUD; hidden when modals open |
| **5** | `DialogueUI`, `ComputerTerminalUI` | `ComputerTerminalUI` closes on `Tab`/`Esc`; `DialogueUI` opts out of `Esc` so interrogations cannot be accidentally aborted |
| **7** | `ArchiveUI`, `TimeAdvanceUI` | `TimeAdvanceUI` closes via `BaseUIPanel`; `ArchiveUI` toggles with `[L]` (and closes via `Esc`) |
| **10** | `InvetoryUI`, `PauseMenu` | `InventoryPanelController` cancels open rename/delete prompts before closing; `PauseMenu` opens only when stack is empty |
| **20** | `InspectModeUI`, `SettingsPanel` | `InspectModeController` discards active note edit on `Esc` before closing; `SettingsPanel` returns to caller |
| **30** | `ReadModeUI` | Closes back cleanly via `BaseUIPanel` |
| **98** | `ScreenFader` | Non-blocking fade overlay (`picking-mode: ignore`) |
| **99** | `SaveIndicatorUI` | Always-on-top saving/loading status pill |

> **Table 8.X — `UIDocument` Sorting Order Stack and Escape Handling in `GeometryAttempt`.**

Focus and player locking are centralized in `BaseUIPanel` and `UIPanelTracker`. Opening any `BaseUIPanel` increments `openModalPanelCount` (`PushModalPanel`) and pushes the panel onto `escapeStack` if `ClosableWithEscape` is true (`DialogueUI` calls `PushModalPanel` directly without joining `escapeStack`). Whenever `openModalPanelCount > 0`, `UIPanelTracker` calls `PlayerController.SetLocked(true)`, freezing locomotion and camera zoom until the last panel pops. `GlobalEscapeManager` acts as the single `Esc` owner: it pops the topmost panel via `RequestEscapeClose()` (allowing `OnEscapePressed()` hooks to cancel sub-prompts such as note editing or folder deletion first) and toggles `PauseMenuController` only when no modal panel is open and the scene is not `MainMenu`. As a defensive safeguard against soft-locks if a panel is ever disabled mid-transition, `UIPanelTracker.CheckAndResetIfStuck()` resets a stuck modal counter after 3 seconds of active unpaused gameplay.

#### 8.2.5 Feedback: What Is Signalled and What Is Withheld

To preserve interpretive ambiguity (Section 8.1.6) without frustrating usability, the interface draws a strict boundary between **procedural legibility** and **epistemic judgment**.

On the procedural side, the interface clearly signals item status and interactive readiness:
- **Unseen evidence:** Newly acquired cards display a warm gold border (`.evidence-card--new`, `rgba(200, 175, 100, 0.90)`), and the Free-Roam HUD swaps the inventory icon (`InventoryNewSprite`) until all new items are opened in Inspect or Read Mode.
- **Pending evidence:** Reports awaiting laboratory processing render at `45%` opacity (`.evidence-card--pending`) behind a dark overlay with an hourglass (`⏳`) and live `Xh Xm` timer, blocking drag, equip, and inspection.
- **Locked world actions:** Unavailable actions remain visible in `INTERACTION` mode with dimmed, red-edged styling (`.question-btn--locked`), while ready actions append `[Use ID]` via `WorldObjectActionData.GetDisplayLabel`.
- **Empty and terminal misses:** Empty dialogue evaluations display neutral text (`"Equip evidence in a slot to start inquiry."`), and mismatched terminal submissions display `"No matching record found for this combination."` without hinting which slot, tape, or document failed.

Within conversation, `DialogueUI` and `ArchiveUI` also record two procedural relevance markers: an out-of-range warning badge (`.badge-warning` / `.out-of-range-badge`, indicating that equipped evidence missed the current chain node’s target and reduced patience) and a slip badge (`.badge-success` / `.slip-badge`, marking that a one-shot `SlipEventData` fired). In `DialogueUI`, these badges are appended with `.hidden` while the conversation stream is collapsed—the default view—and appear only if the player explicitly expands the stream, whereas `ArchiveUI` displays them in the session log. Even here, the distinction remains strict: the interface indicates whether a move was *procedurally relevant* to a character’s active line of questioning or triggered a state transition, never whether the player’s overarching theory of the crime is right or wrong. *[Author note: in `DialogueUI`, confirm whether the slip badge text (`"SLIP EVENT {id}"`) was intentionally left exposed as a procedural cue or should be noted in 8.2.8 as a debug-style string carried into the playtest build.]*

#### 8.2.6 Time and Transitions

Time pressure is communicated through three restrained interface elements rather than intrusive pop-ups:

1. **The Persistent Clock HUD (`FreeRoamHUDController`):** Positioned top-left (`217px` wide in `rgba(14, 12, 9, 0.88)`), the clock displays in-game wall time starting at `3:30 PM`, remaining hours and minutes (`Xh Xm left`), a state label (`RUNNING`, `PAUSED`, `WARNING`, or `EXPIRED`), and a horizontal bar (`clock-bar-fill`). Crossing the 12-hour `warningAtHoursRemaining` mark shifts text and bar accents from muted gold to bright warning amber; hitting `0h` shifts them to red (`EXPIRED`). Crucially, the entire HUD remains hidden until `FLAG_INTERNAL_CLOCK_STARTS` fires, and hides whenever modal panels open or during forced `NPCApproachPlayer` sequences—ensuring the clock is *felt* across exploration rather than stared at over conversations. Below, the HUD displays a minimalist `fr-slot-A` / `fr-slot-B` strip (with grayscale-filtered thumbnails) and bottom-right shortcut badges (`[I]`, `[TAB]`, `[L]`).
2. **The Time-Advance Panel (`TimeAdvanceController`):** Interacting with the Evidence Room chair or Observation Room printer opens a modal `1–24h` integer-snapped slider (`WAIT / REST`). Confirming fades the screen via `ScreenFader` (`1.6s` out/in) while calling `InternalClockManager.SkipHours(hours)`, and rejects requests with an inline `.time-error` message if the clock is stopped or already expired—framing waiting as a deliberate, irreversible commitment.
3. **The Locked-Room Countdown (`LockedRoomSequenceController`):** During the 25-second collapse sequence, an `OnGUI` progress bar interpolates from green to red and pulses rhythmically below `10s`, paired with an `[E] Inject Adrenaline` prompt once `AI` and `AV` are equipped.

#### 8.2.7 Visual Language

Across all UI Toolkit documents, colour and typography are used functionally to distinguish speaker, mode, and epistemic status rather than for ornament. Built on the palette codified in `RootReference.uss`, screens pair deep warm noir surfaces (`#0E0C0A` to `#302C20` in menus and HUDs; charcoal `#202020` / `#1B1B19` / `#111110` in investigative panels) with warm gold headings (`#DCC89B`), muted parchment body copy (`#B9AA87`, `#E8E6DF`), and bevelled keycap badges (`.key-badge` with a `2px` bottom border). Semantic accents consistently encode agency and state: **slate blue (`#4778af` / `#37699e`)** marks Detective Julian Stoker and Inquiry mode; **warm amber (`#af8041` / `#946a34`)** marks witnesses, suspects, and Captain Miller; **magenta (`#904c77`)** marks Confrontation mode; **burnt amber-gold (`#c88c3c` / `rgba(200, 175, 100, 0.90)`)** flags warnings, pending timers, and unseen evidence; and **cyan (`rgba(80, 190, 230, 0.55)`)** isolates computer access tapes.

Typographically, the `ComputerTerminal` panel adopts `AnonymousPro-Regular.ttf` (`10px`–`12px`) to give institutional database queries a monospaced terminal character, while dialogue, inventory, inspect, and archive panels rely on wide-tracked uppercase headers (`1px`–`4px` letter-spacing, `8px`–`11px` micro-badges, `12px`–`16px` readable body text, and `26px`–`52px` display titles) paired with custom `Click.png` and `Grab.png` cursors to reinforce tactile card handling. On the technical side, four core gameplay panels (`ArchiveUI`, `DialogueUI`, `FreeRoamUI`, `ComputerTerminalUI`) share `ScalePanelSettings` and are dynamically rescaled to screen width by `SettingsPanelController.ApplyTargetedUIScaling()`. Because early UI Toolkit compatibility limited CSS custom properties across `.uss` files, the `:root` tokens documented in `RootReference.uss` were inlined directly into each panel stylesheet—a pragmatic trade-off that guaranteed reliable rendering at the cost of manual stylesheet synchronization.

#### 8.2.8 Interface Limitations and Summary

Taken together, the interface translates the thesis’s core mechanics into tangible interactions—turning slots into conversational instruments, folders and notes into externalized theory-building tools, and the HUD clock into felt *kairos* pressure—while strictly withholding confirmation of whether the player’s final deduction is right. Four implementation limitations remain in the evaluated build:

1. **Legacy `OnGUI` overlays:** Proximity `[E]` prompts, `SlipEventHandler` toasts, `LockedRoomSequenceController`’s 25-second countdown bar, `EndingSequenceController` quotes, and `EndingOutcomeController`’s illustration and credits roll still render via immediate-mode `OnGUI` rather than UI Toolkit.
2. **Inlined stylesheet colours:** Duplicating `RootReference.uss` palette values inline across twelve panel `.uss` files makes global theme adjustments manual.
3. **Partial UI scaling coverage:** Only the four `ScalePanelSettings` panels (`ArchiveUI`, `DialogueUI`, `FreeRoamUI`, `ComputerTerminalUI`) scale dynamically via `SettingsPanelController` in `GeometryAttempt` (leaving `InventoryPanel`, `InspectMode`, and `ReadMode` on fixed `PanelSettings`, and leaving the `Scalable UI Documents` list empty in `MainMenu`).
