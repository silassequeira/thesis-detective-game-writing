# 3. Methodology

## 3.1 Research Objectives

Objective 1: Designing for Epistemic Agency

To investigate how a game system can support player agency through epistemic reasoning such as deduction, interpretation, and logical inference, while examining whether these forms of mechanical agency can coexist with emotional engagement and narrative coherence in a story-driven experience.

- Objective 1.1: To explore how free spatial exploration and the presence of multiple solutions to narrative obstacles affect the player’s ability to approach investigative problems creatively and develop a personal sense of agency.

- Objective 1.2: To investigate how allowing players to construct their own theories and accusations, including the possibility of incorrect or premature conclusions, shapes narrative progression, perceived responsibility, and the range of possible outcomes.

Objective 2: Developing an Easy-to-Learn Evidence System

To investigate whether a two-slot evidence combination system can encourage intentional logical reasoning and understanding of the mystery while remaining accessible, and whether it avoids the exact-match friction of parser-based systems or rigid contradiction mechanics present in traditional detective games.

- Objective 2.1: To explore how integrating this system with multiple forms of interaction enables different investigative strategies and detective styles, and whether it allows players to reach similar narrative goals through distinct logical paths.

Objective 3: Modeling Social Negotiation Dynamics

To investigate how a social interaction system based on patience, and conversational constraints can function as a primary investigative mechanic, requiring players to navigate social positioning alongside evidence gathering.

- Objective 3.1: To examine whether a procedural approach to dialogue and interaction can provide meaningful player feedback and perceived choice while avoiding exponential narrative branching, and whether this approach maintains narrative clarity and consequence.

Objective 4: Integrating Surreal Narrative with Mechanics

To explore how dream sequences can operate as a dual-purpose system that provides emotional and thematic depth while also guiding player interpretation and influencing investigative reasoning in the waking state.

- Objective 4.1: To investigate how the development of parallel character arcs for the detective and the missing character enhances both epistemic engagement and narrative depth, and whether this structure fosters emotional investment and guides players toward more meaningful investigative conclusions.

## 3.2 Design Research Approach 

This project follows a Research through Design (RtD) methodology, in which knowledge is generated through the iterative creation and reflection upon a designed artifact. Rather than validating a hypothesis through empirical testing alone, the research advances its argument by building, testing, and analyzing a concrete system that embodies the proposed theoretical framework.

Within this Research through Design framework, the designed artifact functions as a research instrument rather than a final product. Knowledge is generated through the tensions, failures, and affordances revealed during the iterative design and testing of the prototype. The focus is not on validating a singular correct solution, but on articulating design insights regarding epistemic agency, investigative interaction, and the integration of symbolic narrative systems into gameplay mechanics.

## 3.3 Process

In the initial phases, theoretical research and case studies were used to diverge across existing models of narrative agency, mystery structures, and player interpretation. These insights were then synthesized into a set of design principles centered on epistemic agency, which guided the definition of the core design problem.

Subsequent phases involved the development of interactive prototypes as experimental artifacts. These prototypes were not intended as finished products, but as tools for inquiry, allowing the research to test how different informational structures shaped player interpretation, deduction, and commitment to a theory of truth. Iterative playtesting and reflective analysis informed successive design decisions, enabling theory and practice to co-evolve throughout the project.

#### 1st Semester

A1. State of the Art Study

To establish a theoretical and historical foundation for interactive narrative agency by critically reviewing theater theory, ergodic literature, interactive fiction and narrative videogames, resulting in a structured literature review chapter.

A2. Survey of Design Case Studies

To analyze how agency is implemented in existing interactive detective and narrative systems by examining representative games and media, producing a comparative case study analysis informing design decisions.

A3. Design Proposal

To define the conceptual, narrative and interaction goals of the project by synthesizing theoretical insights into a coherent design vision, resulting in a formal design proposal for an interactive narrative system.

#### 2nd Semester 

A4. Concept Rehearsal Mockup 

To explore narrative structure, player agency and interaction flow through low-fidelity representations and sketches, producing a conceptual mockup that tests core ideas before implementation.

A5. Early Gameplay Rehearsal Workshops

To evaluate narrative clarity and player agency through guided rehearsal sessions and discussion, generating qualitative feedback that informs iterative refinement of the design.

A6. Digital Prototyping

To implement the core mechanics, narrative systems and interaction models in a functional digital prototype, producing an interactive version of the proposed design suitable for testing.

A7. Playtesting

To assess usability, narrative coherence and perceived agency through structured playtesting sessions, resulting in documented observations and identified design issues.

A8. Game Design Synthesis

To consolidate insights from prototyping and playtesting into a refined narrative and interaction system, producing a cohesive and validated game design framework.

A9. Reflections and Design Insights

To critically reflect on the design process and outcomes in relation to theory and research questions, generating articulated design insights and contributions to interactive narrative design.

A10. Dissertation Writing

To document the research process, theoretical framework, design methodology and findings in a coherent academic thesis, resulting in the final dissertation submission.
