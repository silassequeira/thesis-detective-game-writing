##### _Detective Stoker,_

Dutch Thorne’s investigation was left incomplete, so you might still need to request and gather more evidence in the form of reports, and you’ll do that by requesting any evidence that you require that isn’t currently present in the computer database._ 

_To ensure this case sticks, you are required to follow the Standardized Interrogation and Evidence Processing Protocols outlined below. Treat this as the official rulebook for how you handle every interaction in this investigation. Follow it to the letter._ 

Your main tool is not a gun or a dialogue tree – it is how you **combine evidence** and **intent** . 

Everything you do in this case flows through three ideas: 

- Choose **who** you talk to or **what** you interact with. 

- Choose **what** you load into your two evidence slots. 

- Choose **how** you use it: Inquiry, Confrontation, or Leave. 

##### **1. MANDATORY EVIDENCE SLOT PROTOCOL (TWO-PIECE RULE)** 

You always have up to **two slots** available when interacting. 

- Slot A – left hand 

- Slot B – right hand 

You can: 

- Equip **one** piece of evidence. 

- Equip **two** pieces of evidence that make sense together. 

If Slot A and Slot B **support the same idea** , they become a stronger argument. 

If they **contradict each other** or don’t fit the situation, you break your logic chain and test the NPC’s patience. 

##### **2. STANDARDIZED INTERVIEW CLASSIFICATION (INQUIRY VS. CONFRONTATION)** 

When you open a conversation with a suspect or the captain, you choose an **Intent** : 

- **Inquiry** – soft questioning 

Use this when you want to ask, clarify, or probe. Good for building a chain of understanding and testing reactions. 

- **Confrontation** – hard pressure 

Use this when you want to call out a lie, break an alibi, or force a confession. Best used after a sequence of good Inquiries. 

- **Leave Conversation** – step back 

Use this to walk away before you burn patience. You can return later with better evidence. 

##### **2.1. How to perform an Inquiry** 

1. Choose your target (Elara, Kae, Captain Vance, Buckley). 

2. Equip 0–2 relevant pieces of evidence. 

3. Select **Inquiry** . 

4. Ask something that logically connects to what you’re showing. 

Good Inquiries: 

- Use **relevant** evidence (the NPC can know about it or be involved in it). 

- Line up in a **logical sequence** (each question follows from the last). 

- Build an invisible **logic chain** : each successful Inquiry makes your next Confrontation stronger. 

Bad Inquiries: 

- Use random or unrelated evidence. 

- Jump topics wildly. 

- Present things the NPC has no way to know about. 

Too many bad Inquiries: 

- Break your logic chain. 

- Reduce the NPC’s patience. 

- Risk Vance cutting the interrogation off. 

##### **2.2. How to perform a Confrontation** 

Use Confrontation when you think you can **prove** something: 

5. Build up with a few solid Inquiries first. 

6. Equip 1–2 **strong** and **coherent** pieces of evidence. 

7. Select **Confrontation** . 

8. Aim at a **specific claim** or lie. 

Outcomes: 

- **Hit:** Your evidence fits, the NPC’s guard drops, and they reveal something they were holding back. 

- **Miss:** You push a weak or irrelevant argument, they get annoyed, and patience drops hard. 

- **Logic Break:** You present contradictory or nonsense evidence. The interrogation collapses and you may be thrown out. 

##### **2.3. Catching Lies** 

When an NPC says something that **directly contradicts** your evidence, you can use the two-slot system as a reality check. 

Example pattern: 

- Hear a statement: “It’s raining outside.” 

- Remember a document: Employment report that proves that it’s not. 

- Equip the document. 

- Use **Confrontation** . 

If the contradiction is clean and the evidence is strong, the NPC’s **guard drops** , making later Confrontations much easier, and they might even reveal information. 

##### **2.4. Resuming an Interrupted Line of Questioning** 

You will not always finish a chain in one sitting. You may run low on patience, you may need to step out and pull a report first, or you may simply want to sit on a theory overnight. When you do, the thread is **not** lost. 

- Your case file keeps a **bookmark** on every line of questioning you began and did not finish, being tracked separately for each subject. You can see the history of all conversations in the Archive Panel. 

- Walk into an interview with **both slots empty** , and those bookmarks surface at the top of your options as: _"Let's get back to [topic]. [your last question]."_ 

- Selecting one puts you back on the **exact question where you stopped** . The system automatically reloads the evidence you were working with at that moment, so your argument resumes where it stood rather than starting cold. 

- These options only appear when you carry **no evidence** . If you have something equipped, you are opening new ground, not returning to old. 

- Expect a **different answer** the second time through. A subject who has already heard your question will not react the same way twice. 

**Practical rule:** _Leave Conversation_ is a pause, not a surrender. Step out, read your files, request what you are missing, and return to pick the thread back up. 

##### **3. HOW TO THINK WHILE YOU PLAY** 

- Before you slot anything, ask: **“What am I trying to test or prove?”** 

- Use **Inquiry** to explore, **Confrontation** to commit. 

- Make sure two-slot combinations form a **single coherent idea** , not two unrelated ones. 

- When in doubt, step away, read your files, and then come back. 

If you treat every interaction as “I am testing my theory against the world,” the system will feel natural, and the case will genuinely be _your_ investigation. 

##### **4. ELECTRONIC RECORDS REQUEST PROTOCOL (THE COMPUTER SYSTEM)** 

The computer terminal is used to request forensic reports and cross-reference evidence. Think of it as a strict Records Clerk: you must provide the correct **department tape** and **query evidence** to get results. 

##### **Step-by-step:** 

1. Equip a department tape in one slot: 

   - **FL** – Forensics Lab (Ballistics, DNA profiles, GSR, Fingerprint Analysis…) 

   - **MU** – Medical Unit (Autopsy reports, Hospital Admissions…) 

   - **CSU** – Controlled Substances Unit (Toxicology reports, Narcotics Analysis…)

2. Equip the specific evidence you want analyzed in the other slot

##### **6. PATIENCE & COOLDOWN** 

Suspects have limited patience. Breaking logic chains or presenting weak evidence reduces it. 

- **Depleted patience** ends the conversation 

- **Cooldown:** After patience is depleted, a cooldown period begins 

- **Restoration:** Once cooldown completes, patience is restored 

Use the **Wait / Rest** option (available in some of the chairs) to advance time if you need to wait for cooldown or pending evidence.

###### **1. SUBJECT IDENTIFICATION** 

**Provisional Name:** Somnoryl-μ 

**Research Code:** ORX-417 

**Pharmacological Class:** Sub-analgesic μ-opioid analog with atypical oneirogenic and parasomniac properties 

**Intended Indication (Original):** Severe treatment-resistant insomnia; palliative sedation; trauma-associated sleep disorders 

**Current Status:** Withdrawn from all clinical trials. Manufacture, possession, and administration prohibited outside approved containment studies. 

###### **2. SUMMARY FINDINGS** 

Somnoryl-μ (ORX-417) is a synthetic opioid analog demonstrating standard μ-opioid receptor affinity alongside unanticipated and persistent alterations to sleep architecture, most notably REM-dominant sleep states with preserved metacognitive awareness. 

Initial trials suggested promising sedative efficacy. However, longitudinal observation revealed progressive destabilization of wake–sleep boundaries, development of pathological dream attachment, and severe withdrawal syndromes combining opioid dependence with REM rebound phenomena. 

The compound presents unacceptable risks to cognitive autonomy and identity continuity. 

###### **3. MECHANISM OF ACTION (CURRENT MODEL)** 

_Note: Mechanism remains partially theoretical._ 

- Primary binding to μ-opioid receptors produces negligible analgesic effect, emotional attenuation, and attachment reinforcement. 

- Secondary interaction observed in sleep-regulatory circuits results in: 

   - Prolonged REM phases 

   - REM persistence beyond normal sleep cycles 

   - REM intrusion during wakefulness 

- Functional imaging indicates suppression of prefrontal reality-monitoring networks concurrent with heightened limbic and emotional memory activation. 

Collectively, these effects produce highly vivid, emotionally salient dream states with subjective realism exceeding waking perception. 

###### **4. CLINICAL PRESENTATION** 

###### 4.1 Acute Effects 

   - Rapid onset sedation 

   - Hypnagogic imagery within minutes of sleep onset 

   - Reports of immersive, coherent dream environments 

   - Preserved sense of agency within dream state 

- 4.2 Subacute Effects (Repeated Administration) 

   - Lucid parasomnia 

   - Dream continuity across separate sleep episodes 

   - Emotional attachment to dream environments or dream-identified persons 

   - Reduced affective response to waking stimuli 

- 4.3 Chronic Use Effects 

   - Progressive preference for sleep states 

   - Diminished motivation for waking activity 

   - Confusion between autobiographical memory and dream memory 

   - Emergence of alternate dream-identity constructs 

###### **5. WITHDRAWAL SYNDROME** 

Withdrawal from Somnoryl-μ produces a compound syndrome combining mild opioid withdrawal symptoms with severe parasomniac rebound. 

Observed Symptoms: 

- Acute insomnia (48 – 96 hours) 

- Intense REM rebound with involuntary dream intrusion 

- Vivid nightmares occurring during wakefulness 

- Emotional flooding (panic, grief, longing) 

- Depersonalization and derealization 

Several subjects reported the subjective belief that waking reality was artificial or incorrect during withdrawal. 

###### **6. RARE AND ANOMALOUS FINDINGS** 

In fewer than 2% of long-term subjects, investigators documented remarkably convergent dream reports between individuals with prolonged exposure histories. 

Observed similarities included: 

- Identical spatial descriptions 

- Shared symbolic figures 

- Overlapping narrative progression 

No conclusive mechanism has been identified. Competing hypotheses include: 

- Expectation-driven narrative convergence 

- Memory contamination 

- Sleep-cycle neural entrainment 

Investigators note that in rare cases subjects involved maintained conviction of shared experience despite contradictory evidence. 

###### **7. ETHICAL AND SOCIETAL CONCERNS** 

The primary risk of Somnoryl-μ is not toxicity but volitional disengagement from waking life. 

Key concerns: 

- Loss of informed consent capacity 

- Impaired legal testimony reliability 

- Persistent non-participation in social structures 

- Resistance to treatment or recovery 

Several subjects expressed explicit refusal to discontinue treatment, citing the dream state as their “primary lived reality.” 

###### **8. TERMINATION OF PROGRAM** 

The ORX-417 program was terminated following unanimous recommendation by the Neuroethics Oversight Committee. 

###### Official Reason for Termination **:** 

_Sustained impairment of reality integration and unacceptable risk to personal autonomy._ 

All remaining compound stocks were ordered destroyed or sequestered under Class-Black containment. 

###### **9. ADDENDUM: INVESTIGATOR NOTE** 

“We did not create a drug that induces sleep. We created a drug that convinces the brain that waking up is a mistake.” 

— Lead Investigator, ORX-417 Program
