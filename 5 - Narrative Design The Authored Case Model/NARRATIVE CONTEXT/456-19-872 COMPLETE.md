1. PURPOSE OF REVIEW

This notice documents command review of Case No. 456-19-872, concerning the disappearance of JULIE ELLISON, and provides direction regarding the continuation of active investigative activity.

The case was reviewed following initial missing-person intake, travel verification, welfare-check activity at Mirror Lake Motel, witness interviews, and supplemental assessment by assigned investigator Detective Elias THORNE.

2. ESTABLISHED FACTS

The following facts have been established through available reporting and records:

1. ELLISON is an adult missing person.
2. ELLISON departed her shared residence without reported signs of forced entry, physical struggle, theft, or property damage.
3. ELLISON removed selected personal belongings consistent with travel.
4. ELLISON left a handwritten note requesting that reporting party WHITFIELD not attempt to locate or follow her.
5. ELLISON researched Mirror Lake before her departure.
6. ELLISON reserved and paid for lodging at the Mirror Lake Motel under her own name.
7. ELLISON was confirmed to have checked into the Mirror Lake Motel without an identified companion.
8. No witness has established that ELLISON was visibly injured, threatened, restrained, or forced to travel.
9. No confirmed sighting establishes ELLISON’s location after her motel check-in.
10. No identified suspect, crime scene, physical evidence, or verified criminal act has been developed.
3. INVESTIGATIVE LIMITATIONS The investigation has been materially limited by the absence of reliable evidence establishing criminal involvement.

The motel room registered to ELLISON, Room 114, was cleaned and reassigned before a complete forensic examination could be requested. Motel staff disposed of bedding, refuse, disposable items, and loose paper material through routine housekeeping procedures. The content, ownership, and possible evidentiary value of those materials cannot now be independently verified.

Available witness accounts establish that ELLISON arrived in Mirror Lake voluntarily. They do not establish whether her later absence was voluntary, whether she met another person, or whether she left the Mirror Lake area.

Information supplied by WHITFIELD concerning ELLISON’s recent improvement in mood, sleep, recurring dreams, and references to an unidentified male remains uncorroborated. No physical evidence, medical record, witness identification, or identifiable third party has been developed from those concerns.

4. COMMAND REVIEW

Detective THORNE’s supplemental memorandum correctly identifies unresolved circumstances surrounding ELLISON’s disappearance after her arrival in Mirror Lake.

However, the information currently available does not establish probable cause or reasonable grounds to classify this matter as:

- Abduction
- Unlawful confinement
- Assault
- Drugging or involuntary impairment
- Financial exploitation
- Homicide
- Any other identifiable criminal offence

The fact that the subject remains unlocated is noted. It does not, in itself, establish that a criminal act occurred.

The department has exhausted reasonable initial measures available under the current classification, including residence review, travel verification, motel contact, limited local inquiry, witness interviews, and records requests.

No further active deployment of Fourth District personnel is authorized at this time absent new, credible, and verifiable information.

MISSING ADULT — INACTIVE / PENDING CREDIBLE NEW INFORMATION

The information below does not independently establish abduction, unlawful confinement, assault, drugging, or other criminal conduct. It is retained because the totality of the reporting party’s account, the subject’s documented travel, and the incomplete condition of the Mirror Lake Motel location leave the circumstances of the subject’s disappearance unresolved.

2. VOLUNTARY TRAVEL VERSUS VOLUNTARY DISAPPEARANCE

Current evidence supports the conclusion that ELLISON prepared for and travelled to Mirror Lake by apparent choice.

The subject:

- Conducted apparent research regarding Mirror Lake before departure
- Obtained or accessed travel directions
- Reserved lodging at the Mirror Lake Motel under her own name
- Removed limited personal belongings from her shared residence

They do not establish that ELLISON’s subsequent absence from the motel, the town, or known contacts was voluntary. 3. REPORTED CHANGE IN SUBJECT BEHAVIOUR

Reporting party WHITFIELD described ELLISON as having a long-standing history of disturbed sleep, recurring nightmares, emotional distress, and periods of withdrawal.

WHITFIELD described a substantial behavioural change during the approximately one to two weeks preceding ELLISON’s departure. According to WHITFIELD, ELLISON appeared calmer, more rested, more optimistic, and more socially engaged than she had been for an extended period.

WHITFIELD specifically stated that ELLISON claimed her nightmares had ceased and that she was sleeping through the night.

No independent medical, psychological, or toxicological assessment of ELLISON was available after the behavioural change was reported.

VERBATIM ACCOUNT As provided by witness WHITFIELD:

“Julie has had a difficult life. I know that sounds vague, but she has been dealing with things for a long time. She used to have nightmares almost every night. Not normal bad dreams. She would wake up crying, screaming sometimes, or just sit up and stare at the wall like she was still somewhere else.

I asked her more than once to talk to somebody about it properly, but she never wanted to. She said people only wanted to make her remember things she was trying to forget. Some nights she barely slept at all. Other nights, she slept for hours and still woke up exhausted.

She started waking up early, making coffee, cleaning things around the apartment. Small things, but things she had not wanted to do for a long time.

I told her that maybe she had seen it in a film, or online, or in one of her dreams. She looked at me strangely and said, “I have been there. Just not the way you mean.” I did not know what to say to that. I thought she was being dramatic, or maybe she was confused.

But she was not acting confused. She was acting certain. That is what scared me.

She also mentioned a man. I never met him. I never saw him call her. I never saw messages from him. She said she knew him from her dreams. I asked her his name, and she would not tell me. She said that names were not important where he was.

I told her that it was not normal to trust somebody she had only dreamed about. She got angry with me. Not shouting angry. More like I had insulted someone she cared about. She said he understood her, and that he knew what she had been through without making her explain it.

The last time I saw her, she seemed peaceful. That is the wrong word, maybe, because she did not seem happy exactly. She seemed like she had already made a decision and there was nothing I could say to change it.

I went to sleep that night. When I woke up, she was gone. Some of her clothes were gone, her bag was gone, and there was a note for me.

The note said not to look for her. It said she was sorry and that she was going somewhere she needed to be. It did not say where. It did not say when she would come back.

I know how this sounds. I know she left on her own. I know there was no fight here, and nobody broke in. But I do not think she just ran away. I do not think she was herself at the end.

She kept saying that she was finally able to rest. Julie never rested. Not really. Not until those last few nights. Then she left everything behind like she did not need any of it anymore.

Please do not just mark this as someone choosing to disappear. Something happened to her. I do not know what it was, but something changed her before she left.”
