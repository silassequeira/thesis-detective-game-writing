## Revised prose

The dialogue system determines which locally authored questions and responses become available within that progression. In the implementation, this local resolution depends on more than the currently equipped evidence. It also considers the character being addressed, whether the player is conducting an Inquiry or a Confrontation, the character’s recorded dialogue history, the active Logic Chain, and the narrative conditions that enable or suppress particular interactions. The relationship between global progression and local dialogue is therefore mediated by persistent state and events, rather than by a single, self-contained conversation tree. The supplied code makes this coordination explicit through the narrative state manager, character knowledge logs, and rule-based dialogue evaluation.

A Logic Chain is best understood as an authored sequence representing a line of investigative reasoning. Each chain belongs to a character, unless deliberately configured otherwise, and contains an entry node and an ordered collection of dialogue nodes. These nodes hold the questions, responses, evidence requirements, and optional narrative events through which the line of inquiry becomes playable. A chain can require particular narrative flags or previously visited dialogue nodes before it becomes available. Consequently, possessing an item of evidence does not necessarily make every related question immediately accessible: the player may also need to establish an earlier premise or bring the investigation into the appropriate narrative state. The chain is therefore not identical to a conversation session. Its progress can persist after the player stops speaking to the character and returns later.

The player engages with this structure by equipping up to two evidence items and approaching a character in either Inquiry or Confrontation mode. Inquiry presents questioning, while Confrontation presents accusatory pressure. These modes do not merely change the presentation of an otherwise identical menu; the evaluator requests nodes and interactions of the selected type. The player can also change the equipped evidence or switch modes during an ordinary conversation, prompting the system to recalculate the available questions. Dialogue availability is therefore continuously reconstructed from the player’s current evidential proposal and the investigation’s accumulated state, rather than fixed when the conversation begins.

Evidence functions here as a semantic key in a precise operational sense: an evidence identifier participates in rules that determine which authored questions can appear. A node’s primary question uses the node’s required evidence, while additional questions use their own evidence triggers and must satisfy the node’s logic-range conditions. Some questions accept any one item from a list. Others require a combination. In the current two-slot implementation, a combination is expressed as an anchor item together with any one permitted partner. A list containing an anchor and several possible partners therefore defines several acceptable pairings, not a requirement to equip every listed item simultaneously. Selecting two pieces of evidence proposes a relation that the system can recognise, but the relation’s wording and consequences remain authored.

For example, a node could be configured so that a weapon alone makes a general question available, while the weapon combined with a witness statement makes a more specific challenge available. This illustrates the implementation’s matching rules rather than a claim about a particular authored scene. The important distinction is between acquiring evidence and deciding how to mobilise it. Acquisition adds an item to the player’s investigative resources; equipping it for a particular character, in a particular mode, tests one of its possible conversational applications. The same item can therefore support different questions across different contexts without the system assigning it one universal conversational meaning.

The available menu is also selective rather than a simple list of every matching question. The evaluator gives chain questions precedence over standalone interactions that overlap with evidence already covered by those questions. Within a node, a more specific combination question can suppress a question supported by a smaller subset of the same evidence. A comparable rule applies between standalone interactions. The remaining options are ordered so that resumption and combination-based chain questions appear ahead of less specific options, while weak questions appear last. These rules make the menu sensitive to the specificity of the player’s proposal and reduce competing formulations of the same evidential material. They do not guarantee that every question supported by either equipped item will remain visible.

Once the player answers an advancing chain question, the system enters that chain if necessary and records the node as visited. Subsequent evaluation uses the chain’s ordered nodes and the character’s history to determine the next relevant stage. During an active chain, other chains for that character do not normally surface as competing lines of inquiry. Progression is recorded at the node level, however, rather than requiring the player to exhaust every question held by a node. An advancing answer establishes that the node has occurred; it is not necessary to ask all of its alternative formulations before moving forward. Questions can also be marked as non-advancing. These produce their authored responses and can trigger narrative events, but they do not enter the chain, mark its node as visited, create a bookmark, or contribute investigative strength. This distinguishes pursuing a line of reasoning from making a supplementary remark within its context.

Interruption does not ordinarily erase this work. Closing a normal conversation clears the character’s active-chain status, but preserves the dialogue records and chain bookmarks. A bookmark stores the last advancing question, its node, its displayed text, and its relevant evidence. When the player returns with no evidence equipped and no chain active, an eligible unfinished chain can offer a resumption option. Selecting it restores the bookmarked evidence to the slots where possible and replays the bookmarked question, allowing the player to re-enter the line of inquiry through an explicit conversational reminder. Resumption remains conditional: suspended or narratively suppressed chains do not offer these options, and the system checks whether the chain has already reached its recorded final node. A chain is therefore persistent, but not unconditionally available.

The system also represents unsuccessful evidential proposals. If an active node has no valid question for the equipped evidence and has an authored weak-question fallback, the system can surface that fallback instead. Some chains explicitly permit this behaviour at their entry point as well. Selecting a weak question does not advance the investigation. It reduces the character’s patience and produces a diminished response, with a second response available for repeated failure. Two consecutive out-of-range attempts break an active chain, while a recorded in-chain answer resets the consecutive-failure count. Patience is maintained separately from chain progress, so breaking a line of questioning does not restore the character’s willingness to engage. Crossing configured low-patience or depleted-patience thresholds can also set narrative flags and notify other systems. The consequence of an unsupported proposal is therefore not simply the absence of progress; it can alter the conditions of subsequent interaction.

A further character-level variable makes investigative pressure cumulative across chains. The implementation calls this value strength, but it represents accumulated argumentative pressure against the character rather than the character’s personal resilience. Each advancing question can contribute an authored amount, counted only once for that node-and-question pair. By default, Confrontation nodes contribute one point and Inquiry nodes contribute none, although individual nodes can override these defaults. Weak questions and non-advancing questions contribute nothing. Repeating the same advancing question therefore cannot repeatedly increase pressure. Because the value belongs to the character’s knowledge log rather than to an individual chain, different lines of inquiry can contribute to a shared threshold. Narrative rules can then use that threshold, together with flag conditions, to make a later sequence eligible.

This provides a more concrete account of character development than describing it as an unauthored breakdown. The character’s responses and threshold-triggered sequences are authored, but the player’s route through the available evidence determines which contributions accumulate and when the relevant conditions are met. A later reaction can therefore acknowledge pressure established across several earlier exchanges without requiring all of those exchanges to belong to one conversation or one chain. The architecture supports a cumulative character arc whose timing depends on investigative activity; it does not independently generate a psychological arc or infer one from the semantic content of the player’s reasoning.

Flags and slip events connect these local exchanges to the wider investigation. Flags represent conditions that can remain active, be queried, and, where appropriate, be cleared. They can enable a chain, condition a question, or activate a suppression rule that removes chains and standalone interactions from consideration. Slip events are registered event identifiers that notify the narrative system when a configured occurrence takes place. Although a slip event can represent an involuntary revelation in the fiction, its technical role is broader: the same event mechanism is also called from narrative pages, choices, object interactions, and evidence-processing completion. It is therefore more accurate to treat a slip as an authored narrative occurrence with possible systemic consequences, rather than assume that every event is automatically produced by accumulated pressure.

The dialogue architecture also includes exchanges that do not belong to Logic Chains. Standalone evidence interactions provide questions and responses without advancing a chain, and may be restricted by character, mode, evidence, or narrative flags. Their questions can be hidden while a chain is active, require an additional flag, or disappear after their initial and optional variation responses have been used. Some interactions also initiate delayed evidence processing, creating evidence that remains pending until the configured time has elapsed. These exchanges support practical investigative actions alongside argumentative progression, allowing dialogue to participate in evidence production as well as evidence interpretation.

Scripted narrative sequences form another distinct layer. These sequences present authored pages, potentially involving several speakers, and may include explicit choices with their own response pages. They hide the evidence slots and disable ordinary closing and mode switching while they play. Pages, choices, and sequence completion can set flags or fire events. Flag-based narrative rules can select these sequences according to narrative conditions and character strength, with priority determining which eligible sequence takes precedence. The implementation thus combines evidence-dependent questioning with more tightly controlled narrative interventions. Not every spoken exchange is a Logic Chain, and not every dialogue choice is derived from equipped evidence.

The implications for epistemic agency follow from this distinction between authored content and state-dependent access. First, the conversational significance of a clue is not exhausted by its acquisition. The player decides where to present it, whether to combine it with another item, and whether to use it in Inquiry or Confrontation. Second, the system retains consequences across exchanges through visited nodes, bookmarks, patience, strength, and narrative state. Earlier questioning can therefore change the conditions under which later questioning occurs. Third, the player’s agency operates within an authored space of recognisable relations. The system does not evaluate arbitrary deductions or generate new interpretations; it recognises configured evidential proposals and makes their associated dialogue and consequences available. Its contribution is to make investigative activity consequential across a persistent structure, rather than to remove authorial control over what those consequences mean.
