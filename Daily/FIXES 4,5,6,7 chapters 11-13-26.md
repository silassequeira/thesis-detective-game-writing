
### Big-picture issues

**1. Chapter 7 doesn’t yet show how it answers Chapter 6.** You say the revision is “a direct response to the workshop findings,” but Table 6.4 lists six implications and Chapter 7 only clearly covers some of them:

- Clear failure messages for Theme 2: partly covered by patience and deflection.
- Routes back from the dream to the waking evidence (Theme 4): not addressed.
- Tools for laying out and revising theories (Theme 1, and the goals at the end of 6.5): no case board or note system appears.
- Diegetic cues for pending reports (Theme 6): not addressed.

A traceability table would fix this: theme, then its implication, then the Chapter 7 design response, then the section where it appears. It’s the clearest way to show the RtD loop working.

**2. How is the final accusation judged in the digital version?** In the rehearsals, you decided as facilitator whether a case was “institutionally sufficient.” Chapter 7 only names “conviction flags” and “Ending & Outcome Logic.” This is the point where the player’s interpretation gets evaluated, so it is arguably the most important design decision for your thesis. It needs its own section in Chapter 7 or 8.

**3. There’s a tension with your core claim that you should face directly.** In 7.3.2 the system shows the questions an evidence pairing allows, and you admit that two players with different intentions get the same questions. A critical examiner could argue the question text is doing interpretive work for the player, which is a mild version of the “static detective” problem you set out to solve. That doesn’t break the design, but your Discussion chapter should address it head on. It would also make a good playtesting question: did the questions put the player’s own reasoning into words, or did they reveal reasoning the player hadn’t done?

**4. The dream’s role is inconsistent across chapters.**

- In 4.5, Contribution 2 tests the dream as “a mechanical system rather than atmospheric flavor.”
- In 5.2.7, the second layer unlocks no clues and changes no verdicts.
- Theme 4 found that the dream lacked a route back to the waking evidence.

You need to decide which claim the thesis makes. A related problem: if the entity controlled Elara’s body, she has even less responsibility than the grounded reading gives her, and that weakens the moral tension you set up in 5.2.6. One paragraph acknowledging this would help.

**5. The red herrings need in-world explanations.** E14R (two figures) and E21R (size-45 boot prints) are labelled red herrings, yet they drive Kae’s final tipping-point chain. In the authored truth only Elara was in the alley, so who made the prints and who placed the 911 call? By your own distinction in 7.1, an unexplained red herring is an inconsistency, not intended ambiguity.
